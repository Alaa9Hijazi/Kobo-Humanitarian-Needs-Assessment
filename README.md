# Exploratory Assessment of Barriers to Higher Education Access Among University Students in Gaza

## 📌 Project Overview

This project presents an exploratory humanitarian needs assessment examining the barriers affecting access to and continuation of higher education among university students in Gaza.

The assessment was designed and implemented using KoboToolbox, with the collected data cleaned, analyzed, and visualized using Power BI.

The project applies key concepts from the Humanitarian Needs Assessment process, including assessment planning, questionnaire design, digital data collection, data quality checks, quantitative analysis, disaggregation, and reporting of findings.

---

## 🎯 Assessment Question

What are the main barriers affecting university students' access to and continuation of higher education in Gaza?

### Decision Need

The assessment aims to identify the most significant barriers and priority support needs that may affect students' ability to continue their education.

---

## 👥 Target Population

The target population was university students in Gaza.

A total of 51 responses were collected through a digital questionnaire developed and administered using KoboToolbox.

The sample was exploratory and non-probability based; therefore, the findings should not be interpreted as statistically representative of all university students in Gaza.

## 🔎 Assessment Methodology

The assessment followed a structured humanitarian needs assessment approach:

### 1. Understanding the Assessment
- Defined the assessment question
- Identified the decision need
- Defined the target population
- Identified key information gaps

### 2. Design & Planning
- Developed a quantitative digital questionnaire
- Identified key indicators related to education access and continuation
- Applied question validation and structured response options

### 3. Data Collection
- Collected responses digitally using KoboToolbox
- Used structured questions to facilitate quantitative analysis
- Monitored the collected responses and data quality

### 4. Data Cleaning
The dataset was reviewed before analysis to check for:

- Duplicate responses
- Missing values
- Inconsistent responses
- Data formatting issues
- Response categories
- Open-text responses

No duplicate responses were identified in the final dataset.

### 5. Data Analysis

The analysis focused on:

- Frequencies
- Percentages
- Distribution of responses
- Cross-tabulation
- Identification of high-severity barriers
- Priority support needs

### 6. Visualization & Reporting

The results were transformed into a Power BI dashboard to communicate the main findings in a clear and decision-oriented format.

# 📈 Selected Findings

The assessment identified several important barriers affecting students.

### Internet Access

Among the 51 respondents:

- 19 students reported average internet access.
- 9 students reported poor access.
- 6 students reported very poor access.

Therefore, 15 students (29.4%) reported poor or very poor internet access.

### Electricity Availability

Electricity availability represented a significant challenge:

- 18 students reported a major difficulty.
- 21 students reported a very major difficulty.

This means that 39 out of 51 students (76.5%) reported a major or very major electricity-related difficulty.

### Access to Study Devices

Students reported different levels of access to devices needed for studying:
- 17 students always had access to a study device.
- 22 students had access but with difficulty.
- 12 students did not have access.

This indicates that 34 out of 51 students (66.7%) either lacked a study device or experienced difficulty accessing one.

# ⚠️ Limitations

This assessment has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings.

- The sample size is relatively small (51 respondents).
- The sample was non-probability based.
- The findings are exploratory and should not be generalized to all university students in Gaza.
- The assessment relies on self-reported information.
- The results represent the situation reported by participants during the data collection period.

Despite these limitations, the assessment provides useful exploratory evidence about barriers affecting students' access to and continuation of higher education.
